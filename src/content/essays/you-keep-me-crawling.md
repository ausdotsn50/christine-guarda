Forgive me, Father, for I have sinned. I cleaned the boots of the fallen. I sat with the exiled. I cared for the people you told me needed forgiveness and forgave them against your judgment.

Forgive me, Father, for I have sinned. I questioned your light, for I was blinded. I asked myself that if it was meant to bring me guidance, why must it hurt to look at.

I lived my life begging for your mercy. Praying for forgiveness. Wishing that I were porcelain-clean before I stepped onto your holy grounds. But why must I keep on crawling?

Forgive me, Father, for I have sinned. After the ten Hail Marys and ten Our Fathers, instead of a guilt-free heart, I am filled with emptiness. Do you relish the sight of my suffering? Do you find entertainment in the fact that I purposefully walk on spikes to please you? Is this what it means to be a subject of an all-knowing, all-loving God?

Forgive me, Father, for I will sin. I wish for my knees to not be bruised after they have rested on the cathedral floor for hours. I wish for my heart to stop aching after I have to endure the trials placed before me without warning. I wish for my eyes to stop pouring, hoping for my pain to drown me. I wish to feel. I wish to not feel.

To live isn’t the same as living. As long as I stay believing, as long as I continue confessing, as long as I keep on praying, nothing of my will will be done.

I am sorry, Father, for all my sins.
