I’m young. I just turned 19, and I still have years ahead of me. I have years to discover new wonders, encounter new people, and chance upon new opportunities. I have the future awaiting me with its wondrous unexpectedness, yet I crave the comfort of the past. I lament the times gone by, the hours taken for granted, the minutes misused, and the seconds wasted. I guess, as a way to cope, I choose to believe that the girl laughing just across from her friends and sitting a few feet away from her father in a famous fast-food restaurant almost 11 years ago is not me. We share the same name, but she doesn’t resemble the person that I have become. She exists in her own timeline, just as I exist in mine right now. I believe that at this very moment, as I’m creating this piece of writing, she’s living her life.

She’s dreaming silly dreams, not aiming to change the world but only to experience new things. She’s playing joyfully with her sisters while making use of the available resources at home because she doesn’t have enough toys to fulfill her imagination, and she is satisfied. It’s okay for her to cry in front of her parents, for she’s a kid, and kids do that. She’s not wealthy, but she doesn’t need to worry about the lack of money, for she’s being provided with just enough. She takes what’s given and buys what’s affordable (she doesn’t even really know the meaning of the word yet). She can’t distinguish the difference between a classmate and a friend, for she considers all her classmates as friends. When she says that you are her classmate, then automatically, you’re her friend and not merely an acquaintance that she is required to see every day.

She belongs to a different time that I no longer recognize, for it’s where her father is sitting in front of the TV, doing all the gross things fathers do while watching his action film. She’s not worrying about matters out of her control because she doesn’t need to. She’s all these things; therefore, she’s not me.

I continue to reject the reality that she is me, or rather, that she was me. She is living a life that I am only yearning for. Because the life that she’s living now is my memories. A series of moments that I can’t quite remember but also cannot forget. Events that I will never experience again. All the things she experienced and felt are mine. The decisions that her impressionable mind made are the starting steps of the long journey that I am presently taking.

The foolish side of me is kind of resenting her because she’s not recognizing the value of the life that she’s in. She keeps on dreaming of a future that surpasses her present, while here I am, wishing, hoping, longing, and aching for the past. The more intelligent part of myself wants to apologize to her. I have this guilt firmly sewn in the depths of my being, weighing a ton and confining me. Yes, she is impressionable, ambitious, and sort of idealistic, but she’s a kid, and she’s dreaming of a life so wonderful it hurts. I have failed her. That little girl will not be seeing rainbows and butterflies once she grows up; she’ll be realizing that life doesn’t come easy for little girls.

She’ll learn to silently sob at night to avoid burdening her family. She’ll recognize the flaws of her own being while sitting among her friends. She will lie about what she can and can’t do so that her mother won’t worry about her. She’ll convince herself not to stand in front of a moving car because her problems are not that deep. I want to apologize to her for growing up to be me.

If I am to guess what she’s doing right now, I’ll say she’s having dinner with her family complete and with food that she absolutely loves. While I am here alone, with no food because of the wrong decisions I made with my money, only drinking water to fill my hunger.

I guess I need to stop disappointing that little girl. I need to stop dwelling on my past and actually live in my present so I can give her a better future. As I said, I’m still young, and I just turned 19. There are still numerous steps in this staircase called life. I am willing to tread carefully and watch my steps so that I won’t need to confront that little girl marching her way up the staircase for the first time. I’ll grow up for her.
